## The economic engine
A retailer rents space, fills it with inventory, and earns a margin on throughput. **The entire
business reduces to two questions asked of every square foot**: how much revenue does it
generate, and what did it cost to occupy and stock. Growth therefore has exactly two sources and
they must never be blended:

`Revenue growth = new-space growth (area addition) + like-for-like growth (SSSG)`

Vishal Mega Mart is the corpus's textbook decomposition: 19% revenue CAGR FY25-28E = ~10% CAGR
area addition (~300 net new stores, 12.2 → 16.2 mn sq ft) + low-double-digit SSSG, producing 8%
CAGR in revenue/sq ft to INR 11.7k by FY28. Every number in that sentence is separately checkable.

Two structural features follow:

- **New stores dilute before they accrete.** A store ramps over 2-3 years, so a fast-expanding
  retailer shows depressed blended margins while every individual store improves — the same
  maturity-cohort problem as `hospitals` and `hotels`. Split mature from ramping.
- **Ind AS 116 dominates the reported numbers.** Leases move to depreciation and interest, so
  EBITDA rises and looks structurally better than a pre-Ind-AS peer's. **Never compare EBITDA
  margins across retailers without confirming both are on the same basis**, and prefer
  EV/EBITDA post-Ind-AS with rent-adjusted cross-checks.

## Analysis sequence
1. **Decompose growth into area addition and SSSG**, both as multi-year series. An undecomposed
   revenue-growth number is not an analysis in this sector.
2. **Revenue per square foot, by vintage cohort and by format.** This is the productivity measure
   and it is what a new store must converge toward. Rising revenue/sq ft with flat SSSG means the
   *mix* of stores changed, not that stores improved.
3. **Store economics at the unit level** — capex per store, revenue at maturity, store-level
   EBITDA margin, and payback in years. If the company does not disclose these, build them from
   the area, revenue/sq ft and the store-level cost stack, and label the derivation.
4. **The maturity cohort split.** Stores <1 year, 1-3 years, mature. Then read consolidated
   margin as a weighted result, not as a performance statement.
5. **Category and private-label mix.** Private label is the gross-margin lever (Vishal Mega Mart's
   ~50bps FY25-28E gross-margin expansion is attributed to category mix and higher private-label
   share). Apparel carries the best margin, staples the worst, and the mix moves margin more than
   negotiation does.
6. **Inventory: turns, ageing and markdown.** In apparel this is *the* forensic centre — unsold
   fashion inventory is a deferred loss. Get turns by category and the provisioning policy.
7. **Occupancy cost and lease structure** — rent as % of revenue, fixed vs revenue-share leases,
   average tenure, escalation clauses, and the renewal cliff. Revenue-share leases transfer risk
   to the landlord and deserve credit.
8. **The omni-channel and quick-commerce question**, both directions: e-commerce/own-app share and
   its contribution margin after delivery and returns, and the cannibalisation and price
   transparency risk from quick-commerce. Vishal Mega Mart's own risk disclosure names DMart,
   Reliance, Swiggy and Zomato — the competitive set now spans formats.
9. **Then working capital and ROCE (pre- and post-lease-capitalisation), and the multiple.**

## Signature KPIs
| KPI | Formula | Unit | How to read it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SSSG** | revenue growth of stores open ≥12 months | % | The like-for-like truth. Get the store-inclusion definition and whether it is footfall- or ticket-led. Vishal Mega Mart ran 11.9% / 25.2% / 13.6% across FY22-24 — the FY23 figure is a Covid base effect and must be said | Decks, transcripts |
| **Revenue per sqft** | revenue / average retail area | INR/sq ft/yr | The productivity measure; track by cohort and format. VMM: 8% CAGR to INR 11.7k by FY28E. Rising while SSSG is flat means the store mix changed | Computed from area + revenue |
| **Store count** | operating stores, with gross adds and closures | count | **Net adds hide closures** — always get gross openings and closures separately, plus the area added. VMM: 581 → 1,101 stores, 9.1 → 16.2 mn sq ft | Decks |
| **Store EBITDA margin** | store-level EBITDA / store revenue | % | Unit economics before corporate overhead — the number that decides whether expansion creates value. Split mature vs ramping cohorts | Decks, or derived |
| **Inventory turns** | COGS / average inventory | x | The sector's central working-capital and quality measure. Falling turns with rising revenue means unsold stock, and in apparel that is a future markdown | Computed |

## Supporting KPIs
Retail area (mn sq ft) and area per store; gross store openings and closures; store additions by
city tier (VMM's additions were majority tier-2-and-beyond — a mix statement with margin
consequences); footfall and conversion; average bill value and items per bill; private-label
share; category mix; gross margin by category; rent as % of revenue and the fixed/revenue-share
split; average lease tenure and renewal profile; capex per store and per sq ft; store payback
years; pre- and post-Ind-AS-116 EBITDA (both, always); inventory days and ageing buckets;
markdown/provision as % of inventory; shrinkage; e-commerce and own-app share with contribution
margin; return rate for online; loyalty-programme membership and its revenue share; employee cost
per store; revenue per employee; ROCE pre- and post-lease-capitalisation; net debt including
lease liabilities.
*Jewellery-specific:* studded-jewellery mix (the margin driver), gold-on-lease share and hedging
policy, making charges as % of revenue, old-gold exchange share, inventory in tonnes as well as
value, franchise vs own-store split.

## Standard exhibit set
Revenue growth decomposed into area addition and SSSG · SSSG series with the definition stated ·
store count and area with gross adds and closures shown separately · revenue per sq ft by cohort
and by format · store-level EBITDA margin, mature vs ramping · store unit economics (capex,
mature revenue, EBITDA, payback) · maturity-cohort mix of the store base · city-tier mix of
additions · category and private-label mix with gross margin by category · rent as % of revenue
and lease-structure split · inventory turns and ageing by category · markdown provision trend ·
pre- vs post-Ind-AS-116 EBITDA reconciliation · e-commerce share and contribution margin ·
working-capital days · ROCE pre- and post-lease · EV/EBITDA band · peer table stated on one
consistent lease-accounting basis.

## Valuation convention
**EV/EBITDA on a forward year, post-Ind-AS 116** — because leases dominate the cost structure and
capital employed, and P/E is distorted by the interest-and-depreciation split that lease
capitalisation creates. Publish the peer table on **one stated basis** and reconcile any peer
reporting differently; this is the sector's most common comparability failure.

Where the company is genuinely brand-led rather than space-led (Page Industries is the corpus's
example — a manufacturer-brand with retail distribution, not a retailer), **P/E on forward EPS is
the right convention and the peer set is FMCG, not retail.** Decide which the company is before
choosing, and record the reason: it changes both the multiple and the comparison set.

A **DCF cross-check** is worth running here because store rollout is a capex schedule with a
knowable payback — Vishal Mega Mart's note carries one alongside the target P/E.

*Traps:* (i) comparing EBITDA margins or EV/EBITDA across pre- and post-Ind-AS-116 reporters —
the single most common error; (ii) valuing a mid-expansion retailer on consolidated margins
depressed by ramping stores (use a forward year far enough out, or value cohorts separately);
(iii) capitalising a Covid-rebound SSSG print as the run-rate; (iv) crediting announced store
pipelines before the leases are signed; (v) ignoring lease liabilities in net debt and therefore
in EV; (vi) for jewellery, valuing gold-price-inflated revenue growth as volume growth.

## Divergence cases

*Same verified fact, two defensible readings, and the evidence that settles them. The rule
and the closed vocabulary of conditioning variables are in `docs/OPINION_VS_ANALYSIS.md`
§7. `prompts/33_thesis_synthesis.md` seeds `state/interpretation_ledger.json` from these;
`prompts/34_thesis_redteam.md` checks 16-18 audit the result. A reading that names no
conditioner is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).*

**1. It trades at 18x forward EV/EBITDA; the peer trades at 26x.**
- *Cheap* (`peer_set_choice`).
- *Not comparable* (`accounting_basis`) — one is pre-Ind-AS 116 and the other post. Lease
  capitalisation moves EBITDA and capital employed enough to change a rating, and this is
  the sector's most common comparability failure. Publish the peer table on one stated
  basis and reconcile any peer reporting differently.
- *Discriminator* (`peer_distribution`) — the peer set restated onto a single basis.

**2. The company is classified as a retailer.**
- *Retail peer set, EV/EBITDA* (`peer_set_choice`) — it operates stores.
- *FMCG peer set, P/E on forward EPS* (`peer_set_choice`) — where the company is genuinely
  brand-led rather than space-led it is a manufacturer-brand with retail distribution.
  Page Industries is the corpus's example. Decide which the company is *before* choosing:
  it changes both the multiple and the comparison set.
- *Discriminator* (`disclosed_mechanism`) — where gross margin is actually earned. Own-brand
  manufacture, or space productivity.

**3. Same-store sales growth is 8%.**
- *Healthy underlying demand* (`growth_durability`).
- *Inflation and area creep* (`capital_intensity`) — SSSG flatters when mature stores add
  trading area or when realisation rises without footfall.
- *Discriminator* (`disclosed_mechanism`) — SSSG decomposed into footfall, conversion and
  average ticket.

## Forensic screens (sector-specific)
- **Net store adds quoted without closures.** A retailer opening 100 and closing 30 has a
  different story from one opening 70.
- SSSG definition changed, or the qualifying store base redefined, between periods.
- Revenue per sq ft rising only because low-productivity stores were closed — a mix effect
  presented as improvement.
- **Inventory turns falling while revenue grows**, especially in apparel; ageing buckets not
  disclosed; markdown provision policy changed or provisioning rate falling as ageing worsens.
- Pre-Ind-AS EBITDA quoted in one period and post-Ind-AS in another; rent reclassified between
  "other expenses" and lease depreciation/interest.
- Store pre-opening and fit-out costs capitalised beyond the fit-out itself.
- Same-store growth carried by price/ticket while footfall falls — check both.
- Franchise or wholesale revenue counted in SSSG.
- Related-party lease arrangements with promoter-owned property; related-party sourcing.
- Loyalty-programme liabilities under-provided; gift-voucher breakage recognised aggressively.
- For jewellery: gold-on-lease and unhedged gold inventory presented as inventory rather than a
  commodity position; revenue growth that is gold price, not volume; making-charge realisation
  falling while revenue rises.

## Dependencies to map
Urban discretionary income and consumer credit · festive and wedding seasonality (Q3 dominance,
and a shifted festive calendar can move a quarter) · mall supply and rental cycles in the target
micro-markets · commercial-property rent inflation · GST rates on apparel and footwear (the
slab-change debates directly reprice the value segment) · cotton and yarn prices for
vertically integrated apparel (link to `fmcg`'s input logic and to K.P.R. Mill / Indo Count) ·
import duty and FTA changes on finished apparel · quick-commerce and e-commerce competitive
intensity and platform commissions · gold price, import duty and hallmarking rules for jewellery ·
minimum-wage and shop-establishment regulation for store payroll · Ind AS 116 and any future lease
standard change.

## Common archetypes here
`capex-to-cashflow` (store rollout maturing — the most common and the one whose maturity-cohort
arithmetic must be shown), `margin-expansion` (private label and mix), `market-share-gainer`
(organised taking share from unorganised — verify with third-party category data),
`quality-compounder` for genuine brand-led names, `garp`, and `turnaround` for
format repairs. Treat `re-rating` with the standard skepticism weight, and be alert to
`cyclical-peak` when a Covid-rebound or festive-shifted SSSG print is doing the work.
