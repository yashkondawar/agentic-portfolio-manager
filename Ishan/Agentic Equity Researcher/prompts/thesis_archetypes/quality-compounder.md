# Archetype — Quality compounder

**Skepticism weight: 2 (low-medium).**

## Definition
A business earning returns well above its cost of capital, able to reinvest a meaningful
share of earnings at similar returns, for long enough that compounding does the work.
The multiple is expected to stay high rather than expand.

## Return source
Earnings/book growth at a sustained high ROCE. Explicitly **not** the multiple — a
compounder thesis that needs the multiple to rise is a `re-rating.md` thesis.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The return record spans a full cycle.** High ROCE for three good years is a cycle,
   not a franchise. Show 8–10 years including a downturn.
2. **The moat is named and mechanically explained.** Brand, distribution density,
   switching cost, regulatory licence, scale economics, network. Which one, and what
   number demonstrates it.
3. **Reinvestment runway exists and is quantified.** Compounding needs somewhere to put
   the money: unpenetrated geographies, categories, capacity. State the runway in years
   or in units, and the incremental ROCE on recent capex — not the blended ROCE, which
   hides deterioration.
4. **Capital allocation history is clean.** Past acquisitions and diversifications
   earning their cost of capital. This is where most "compounders" fail.
5. **The multiple is not being expanded.** The forecast holds the multiple flat or
   compresses it. If it expands, decompose and check the 40% rule.

## Standard evidence pattern
Decade-long ROCE/RoE series through a downturn → the moat metric (market share stability,
pricing realised through an input spike, retention, cost-of-funds advantage) →
incremental ROCE on the last three years' capex → reinvestment runway → the multiple held
constant in the target.

## Standard failure mode
**Paying any price for quality.** The business is genuinely excellent, the multiple is
already at the top of its band, and the forecast return is nearly all multiple
maintenance. Second failure: **blended ROCE masking a deteriorating incremental ROCE**, so
the compounding has already stopped while the average still looks good.

## Falsifiers
- Incremental ROCE on recent capex materially below the historical blend.
- Market share loss in the core category for two consecutive years.
- An acquisition outside the moat's perimeter.
- The reinvestment runway closes and payout rises — the thesis becomes an annuity, worth
  a lower multiple.

## Sectors where it recurs
FMCG, private banks with a deposit-franchise advantage, hospitals with mature clusters,
specialty chemicals under multi-year contracts, exchanges and depositories, IT services.

## Corpus examples
- **HDB Financial Services** (ICICI Securities, Mar-2026) — a well-formed instance in the
  lender idiom. Condition 1 is met with a decade of ~15% average RoE and ~2% average
  credit cost across cycles; condition 2 with a quantified cost-of-funds advantage
  (lowest among peers) and ~80% direct sourcing; condition 5 holds because the target of
  3x Sep'27E BVPS is close to the prevailing multiple, so the return is BVPS growth, not
  re-rating. Its honest weak spot is published in the note itself — slower AUM growth than
  peers over three years (`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §7.3).
