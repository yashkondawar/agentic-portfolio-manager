# Archetype — De-rating (bear)

**Skepticism weight: 4 (high) — the symmetric twin of `re-rating.md`, and held to the
same standard.**

## Definition
The multiple is too high and will fall, independently of earnings. Return is negative
through multiple compression.

## Return source
The multiple. Triggered whenever the return decomposition attributes **>40%** of the
expected (negative) return to multiple change.

## Why it carries the same bar as re-rating
It is the same epistemic claim with the sign flipped: an assertion about other investors'
future opinion, with no internal evidence that can ever confirm it. Applying a hard test
to bullish re-rating arguments and a soft one to bearish de-rating arguments would just
relocate the bias. The corpus offers no template — one REDUCE in 34 notes — so the
discipline has to come from us.

## `must_be_true` — all five
1. **The current premium is measured**, against a comparability-adjusted peer set and
   against the company's own band in standard-deviation terms.
2. **The reason the premium exists is named.** Premiums are usually earned: growth, ROCE,
   franchise durability, scarcity, index weight, low float. State which.
3. **A mechanism removes that reason, and it is company-specific.** Growth decelerating
   below the threshold that justified the premium; ROCE falling as reinvestment runway
   closes; a moat metric deteriorating; the scarcity ending as peers list.
4. **The mechanism is dated and observable.**
5. **What the market currently believes, and why it is wrong, is stated.** A de-rating
   call is a disagreement with the marginal holder.

## Standard evidence pattern
Multiple band with the dates and causes of both extremes → the metric that earned the
premium, trending → peer multiples on a like basis → the dated event that breaks it →
the multiple that then applies, anchored to a comparable that already sits there.

## Standard failure mode
**"It's expensive" as a thesis.** Expensive things stay expensive for long periods, and
in a market with persistent flows into quality, an unearned de-rating call is a
recurring way to be wrong. Second: **confusing a high multiple with a high valuation** —
a 40x business growing 30% with 30% ROCE may be cheaper than a 12x business growing 4%.

## Falsifiers
- The metric that earns the premium keeps delivering.
- The dated catalyst passes and the multiple holds → the premium is structural.
- Peers re-rate up to the target, closing the gap from the other side.
- Earnings disappoint but the multiple *expands* (the market looks through it).

## Practical guidance
Where the de-rating checklist fails but valuation still looks stretched, the honest
output is not a SELL. It is a rating struck on the earnings case with the multiple held
flat, plus an explicit statement that the current multiple is above the historical band
and what that costs the investor if it reverts. That is a disclosed risk rather than an
unevidenced call.

## Sectors where it recurs
Anything that has re-rated hard: quality FMCG, premium private banks, defence and
railways after a policy run, EMS, new-age internet platforms post-listing.
