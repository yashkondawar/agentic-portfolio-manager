# Archetype — GARP (growth at a reasonable price)

**Skepticism weight: 1 (low).** The most honest archetype: the claim is nearly arithmetic.

## Definition
Earnings grow at a rate that is well supported, the starting multiple is not demanding
relative to that growth, and the return is essentially the growth rate. No heroic
assumption about the market's opinion.

## Return source
EPS growth, with the multiple held flat or allowed to compress modestly. If the target
multiple exceeds the current one, decompose and apply the 40% rule.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The growth is driver-backed, not extrapolated.** Volume × realisation, or
   capacity × utilisation, or book × yield — with each driver anchored to guidance
   (credibility-weighted per `prompts/22`), history, or industry data.
2. **The growth rate is achievable against the industry's growth.** Growing at ≥2× the
   end market requires explicit, evidenced share gain — otherwise the forecast is
   arithmetic that the market cannot supply.
3. **The starting multiple is defensible against the growth rate and the return profile.**
   PEG is a crude tool but at least an explicit one; better is the multiple against the
   company's own band and against peers at similar ROCE.
4. **Earnings quality supports the growth being real.** CFO/EBITDA conversion holding,
   working capital not funding the growth, no receivable-days drift.

## Standard evidence pattern
Driver table (volume, price, capacity, utilisation) → industry growth for comparison →
share path if outgrowing → margin path with operating leverage shown → multiple vs own
band and peers → cash conversion check.

## Standard failure mode
**GARP as a label rather than a test.** "Reasonable price" is asserted from a PEG below 1
without asking whether the growth is fundable, whether the margin assumption is in the
historical band, or whether cash follows profit. Second failure: the growth is real but
**bought** — funded by acquisitions or capex that dilute returns, so EPS grows while ROCE
falls.

## Falsifiers
- Two quarters of volume growth below the industry without a stated reason.
- Margin path exceeds the 5-year high without a structural cause.
- Cash conversion deteriorating while reported growth continues.
- Growth financed by equity issuance that offsets the EPS effect.

## Sectors where it recurs
Auto ancillaries, building materials, EMS, mid-cap capital goods, hospitals, diagnostics,
mid-tier IT services, consumer durables.

## Corpus examples
- **Waaree Energies** (Yes Securities, Sep-2025) — 22x FY28E justified by an "implied PEG
  of 0.5x" against strong growth. Condition 3 is addressed explicitly through PEG; the
  note is weaker on condition 2, since module capacity is expanding industry-wide and the
  share path is not fully evidenced.
- **Gravita India** (JM Financial, Mar-2026) — 25x FY28E P/E "recognising that it remains
  in the midst of a multi-year" growth phase; a clean growth-priced-off-forward-earnings
  structure.
