# Archetype — Balance-sheet repair

**Skepticism weight: 3 (medium).**

## Definition
A leveraged company is deleveraging. As debt falls, value transfers from lenders to
equity holders — so equity value can rise sharply even with flat enterprise value.

## Return source
Mechanical: equity = EV − net debt. Falling net debt raises equity value arithmetically.
This is the most *checkable* part of the thesis and the reason the weight is only 3.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The deleveraging path is dated and funded**, from a named source: operating cash
   flow, asset sales, a rights issue, or refinancing. Show the schedule.
2. **The cash flow that repays the debt is not the same cash flow being counted for
   growth.** Double-counting FCF is the standard error.
3. **Covenants and the refinancing calendar clear.** A maturity wall inside the forecast
   period is the thesis's real risk, not the leverage level itself.
4. **The operating business is at least stable.** Deleveraging into a deteriorating
   business is a race, not a thesis.
5. **Dilution risk is quantified.** If repair plausibly needs equity, the share count in
   the target must reflect it.

## Standard evidence pattern
Net debt walk by year → maturity profile → covenant headroom → asset-sale pipeline with
realised comparables → interest cost saving → EPS effect of the interest saving separated
from operating improvement.

## Standard failure mode
**Counting the same rupee twice** — once as FCF funding growth capex and again as debt
repayment. Second: **assuming asset sales close at book value** when distressed sellers
rarely do; demand a realised transaction comparable.

## Falsifiers
- An asset sale slips two quarters or prices below the assumed level.
- Net debt/EBITDA fails to fall despite reported profit (working capital absorbing it).
- Refinancing at a higher rate than modelled.
- Equity raised at a price below the target.

## Sectors where it recurs
Real estate, EPC and infrastructure developers, hotels, telecom, sugar, distressed metals,
airlines.

## Corpus example
**SAMHI Hotels** (Yes Securities, Sep-2025) carries this as a secondary leg: a dated gross
debt reduction of INR 3,000mn over FY26-30E funded by operating cash and asset recycling,
with a realised disposal evidencing condition 1's funding source at a known multiple
(`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §7.2).
