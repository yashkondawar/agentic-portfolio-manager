# Archetype — Margin expansion

**Skepticism weight: 3 (medium).** The most-used vocabulary in the corpus (67% of notes),
which is precisely why it needs a test.

## Definition
Revenue grows modestly or normally, but margins rise — through operating leverage, mix
shift, input relief, or cost action — so profit grows much faster than sales.

## Return source
EBITDA/EPS growth driven by the margin delta rather than the revenue delta.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The mechanism is named and one of a short list:** operating leverage on fixed costs,
   mix shift to higher-margin products/geographies, input cost relief, price increase
   held, cost programme, or scale procurement. "Efficiency" is not a mechanism.
2. **The target margin sits inside the historical band, or a structural reason is given
   for exceeding the 5-year high** — commissioned integration, permanent mix change,
   closed high-cost capacity, contract repricing.
3. **The margin bridge is quantified, line by line.** Gross margin, employee cost, other
   opex — each as a % of sales, with the bps contribution of each lever stated.
4. **Operating leverage is evidenced by the incremental margin actually achieved** in
   recent periods, not assumed from a fixed/variable split.
5. **Input relief is separated from structural gain.** If the margin rises because a
   commodity fell, that is a cycle, not an improvement — and it reverses.

## Standard evidence pattern
Common-size P&L over 5–10 years → margin band with highs and lows dated → the bridge
(bps per lever) → incremental margin on recent revenue growth → the portion attributable
to input prices, flagged separately.

## Standard failure mode
**Crediting a commodity cycle as a structural achievement**, then forecasting it forward.
Second: **the everything-improves forecast**, where gross margin, employee cost and other
opex all improve simultaneously with no trade-off named.

## Falsifiers
- Input cost reverses and margin gives back more than the "structural" portion.
- Incremental margin on new revenue below the historical incremental margin.
- Mix shift stalls — the high-margin segment stops outgrowing.
- A&P or R&D cut is doing the work (borrowing from future revenue).

## Sectors where it recurs
Everywhere; most load-bearing in consumer, chemicals, hospitals, IT services, hotels.

## Corpus example
**Max Healthcare** (Nuvama, Sep-2025) handles the price/volume split honestly —
*"Volume to remain key revenue driver… as ARPOB growth to moderate going forward"* — which
is condition 5 done properly: it declines to credit continued price gains.
