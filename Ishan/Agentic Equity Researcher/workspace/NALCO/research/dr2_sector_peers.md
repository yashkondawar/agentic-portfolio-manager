## 1. Answering the routed open questions

### OQ-FUND-07 — External FY2027 consensus/assumptions for LME aluminium + alumina vs management's $2,800-3,000 LME guide
No single "consensus" service was directly queryable in this pass (no Bloomberg/Refinitiv access), but a spread of independent forecasts was assembled:
- **World Bank** (primary, regulator-grade — CMO April 2026): CY2026 avg $3,200/t, CY2027 avg $3,000/t [F-EXT-1200/1201, S1202].
- **SMM** (secondary, PRA): three scenarios, H2 CY2026 $3,600-3,850/t, easing to $2,900-3,200/t in CY2027 [F-EXT-1203, S1203].
- **Goldman Sachs bear case**: as low as $2,400/t by CY2027 if Indonesian supply and demand disappointment coincide [F-EXT-1204, S1204].
- **Reality check**: LME spot already hit $3,538/t on 28-Apr-2026 — above the World Bank's full-year CY2026 average call — implying the World Bank number, if anything, risks understating CY2026 [F-EXT-1202, S1201].

Management's FY2027 metal guide ($3,000-3,100/t per QT-Q4-083, restated here for comparison) sits almost exactly on the World Bank's CY2027 central case and comfortably above Goldman's conditional bear case — **it reads as base-case-to-slightly-conservative, not aggressive**, against the assembled external range ($2,400-3,200/t for CY2027 across sources). No source in this pass suggests management is guiding above the external range.

### OQ-GUI-01 — Is FY26 alumina/aluminium guidance consistent with independent benchmarks, or lagging/leading?
**Consistent, not lagging.** The independent Platts FOB-Australia alumina index — the primary global benchmark, published by the price-reporting agency itself — averaged **$306.91/t in Q1 CY2026** [F-EXT-1206, S1205], sitting inside NALCO's guided $310-320/t band from the orchestrator brief and matching almost exactly the CMD's own public quote of "$320-340/t" for CY2026 given on 10-Feb-2026 [F-EXT-1209, S1207]. The broader 2025 collapse trajectory (start-2025 ~$671/t, -42% by year-end per one aggregator; WA FOB -59% to $330/t by Apr-2025 [F-EXT-1207/1208, S1206]) is directionally consistent with the company's own stated fall from $562→$385→$310-320, though absolute index levels differ slightly by benchmark/timing convention (WA FOB vs China domestic vs India landed are not the same series — this is a **methodological caveat, not a contradiction**). The structural driver management cites — Indonesian refinery ramp — is independently confirmed and quantified: Indonesian metallurgical alumina capacity is set to reach 7 mtpa by CY2026 [F-EXT-1211, S1204], and Indonesian primary aluminium operating capacity is projected to nearly triple from 0.87mt (2025) to 2.51mt (2026) to 3.56mt (2027) [F-EXT-1212, S1228]. **Conclusion: management's price narrative tracks the independent index closely; no evidence of systematic lag or lead.**

## 2. Price context and India demand

- CY2025 alumina collapsed from a ~$671-810/t peak (2024/early-2025) to $310-340/t range by early-mid 2026 on Indonesian and Chinese refinery capacity additions [S1206, S1204]; a further 1.2mt surplus and ~$335/t average is projected for CY2026 by one aggregator's cited forecast panel [F-EXT-1210, S1206] — broadly consistent with, not more bullish than, management's own guide.
- LME aluminium: World Bank $3,200/t (CY2026)/$3,000/t (CY2027); SMM three-scenario range $3,600-3,850/t (H2 CY2026) easing to $2,900-3,200/t (CY2027); Goldman bear case $2,400/t (CY2027). Spot already at $3,538/t (28-Apr-2026), ahead of the World Bank full-year call [S1200-1204].
- **India demand CAGR claim (6.3-7.2% to 2030)**: partially corroborated, not independently falsified. TechSci Research puts the overall India aluminium market at 6.27% CAGR 2024-2030 [F-EXT-1213, S1211] — the low end of, and consistent with, the company's claimed range; a narrower wire-rod sub-segment estimate of 5.93% CAGR [F-EXT-1214, S1212] and other cited market-research houses in the 7.6-7.81% band were also surfaced (sources not individually vetted for methodology). Company's claim sits within a plausible 5.9-7.8% spread across multiple paid-research estimates — **support, not neutral or contradict**, but confidence is capped at "medium" given none of these are official-statistics (no CRISIL/ICRA-branded or government-industry-association figure was located in this pass — flagged as a residual gap below).

## 3. Cost-curve position — company claim only partially verifiable independently

NALCO's claim (per scope brief) to be the world's lowest-cost alumina producer traces to **Wood Mackenzie rankings that are stale in the public record** — the most recent independently-citable instance located in this pass is FY2019 [F-EXT-1215, S1209/S1210]; no current (CY2025/2026) Wood Mackenzie or CRU quartile/decile study naming NALCO specifically was found. Recent press (Dec-2025/Feb-2026) on NALCO's own cost position is qualitative (captive coal ~57% of fuel need, cost benefits from lower external energy dependence) rather than a re-affirmed independent ranking [S1207, S1208]. By contrast, both Hindalco (India upstream, "first decile", EBITDA/t $1,572 FY26, company-characterized) and Vedanta Aluminium ("lower quartile"/"first quartile", cost of production $1,752/t FY26, company-characterized) have **more recent** press citations of their cost-curve position than NALCO does in the material surfaced here. **This is a gap, not a contradiction — recommend logging as an unresolved company_claim** (independently-verifiable current cost-curve ranking for NALCO specifically not located) rather than treating the "lowest-cost" claim as confirmed.

## 5. Regulatory / trade-policy sector context

- **India anti-dumping**: aluminium foil duties (China/Indonesia/Malaysia/Thailand, $619-873/t) extended to 15-Dec-2026 [S1223] — downstream product, limited direct read-through to NALCO's own primary-metal/alumina sales, but relevant domestic value-chain protection context.
- **EU CBAM** (material, new): definitive phase began 1-Jan-2026; first quarterly certificate price set at €75.36/tCO2e on 7-Apr-2026. Indian unwrought-aluminium exports to the EU fell **41.7% YoY** (18,653.8t → 10,874.72t, YTD Jan-2025 vs YTD Jan-2026) [F-EXT-1227, S1224]. This is coal-power-intensity-sensitive — NALCO's captive generation is coal-based, the least CBAM-favorable power source. **This DR2 pass surfaces the external policy/volume fact only; NALCO's own EU export exposure (volume/% of sales) was not sized here** — recommend fundamental-analyst or governance-analyst check company disclosures for EU sales mix to convert this into a company-specific risk estimate.
- **US Section 232**: restructured 6-Apr-2026 to 50% on wholly-metal articles / 25% on derivatives, assessed on full customs value [S1225]. NALCO does not appear to be a significant direct US exporter per documents reviewed to date; relevant mainly as a global trade-flow-diversion/glut risk (metal displaced from the US finding its way to other markets, adding to price pressure) rather than a direct NALCO cost.
- **Coal cost trend**: India e-auction premiums over notified prices rose through FY26 (35% Feb-2026 → 45% Mar-2026, FY26 average 38%, still well below the Q2 FY23 329% peak) [S1226]; Indonesian benchmark thermal coal (GAR 4200kcal) also rose ~33% from start of CY2026 to early April [S1227]. NALCO's ~57% captive-coal fuel share (per earlier press citation) partially insulates it, but the non-captive balance is exposed to this rising-premium trend — a cost tailwind risk worth flagging for estimates-builder.
