The blind spot a global template will always miss in India

Every house above operates as a SEBI-registered Research Analyst, and SEBI's Research Analyst Regulations require every published report to carry a specific disclosure block — disclosure of any financial interest in the subject company, and of actual or beneficial ownership of 1% or more as of the month preceding publication, plus a trading restriction barring the analyst from trading the stock 30 days before and 5 days after the report's publication. Your blueprint doesn't mention this at all — and in India it isn't optional boilerplate, it's what legally separates a research report from a blog post. There's a fitting irony here too: SEBI's late-2024 amendment specifically added a requirement to disclose any use of Artificial Intelligence in preparing a report's analysis — relevant, since your uploaded document (bracketed numbered citations, a closing "want me to go deeper?" prompt, an "AI responses may include mistakes" footer) reads like AI-synthesized content itself.

Architecture: Read both. The docx (sector deep-research prompts) is genuinely sharp in places — the KPI menus per sector are real domain knowledge, not template filler, and the value-chain "bottleneck" framing (spectrum/NPPA/freight) is a good first-principles device. But laid against the PDF prompts and your own stated requirements, there are structural gaps, not just wording nits. I'll go from the highest-leverage fix down to line-level bugs.

You don't have to invent the orchestrator — Anthropic ships a reference pattern for almost exactly this. There's a documented "orchestration mode" build (a Workflow tool that fans a task out to parallel subagents, each returning structured JSON via a report_findings tool — not prose) that maps closely onto what you're describing. Two pieces of it are directly relevant to your open problems:

A verification wave. After the first wave of subagents finishes, a second wave re-runs the same subagent path with an adversarial instruction: re-derive each claim from source and try to refute it, defaulting to "refuted" when uncertain. Both the original result and the verdict get returned together. This is precisely the piece missing from both your documents. Every prompt in Total_Prompts.pdf asserts an audit trail ("every numeric cell must include a Reference ID... source legend must supersede all other context") but nothing checks it — an LLM inventing a plausible page number is exactly as easy as inventing a revenue figure. Right now your pipeline trusts each stage's self-reported citation. Add a cheap verification pass that takes every [FN1]-style reference, looks up what it claims to point to, and cross-checks that against the actual langextract-grounded span (character offsets, not vibes) — anything that doesn't match gets flagged unverified, not silently kept. This is the one addition that would most change whether this survives contact with a real benchmark report.

Journaling + failure isolation. A content-addressed cache (hash the prompt, store the result) so a run that dies at subagent 40 of 60 doesn't cost you the first 39 again, and one broken subagent degrades to an error string instead of ending the whole run. Given how many calls this pipeline makes (16 source docs × extraction, up to 16 peers × sector KPIs, 8 analysis prompts), you will hit partial failures regularly — build for resumability from the start rather than after the first expensive re-run.

Worth reading directly: platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/mid-conversation-effort-example.

On model mechanics specifically (this affects how you wire the tiers): the "effort" parameter (low/medium/high/xhigh) combined with thinking: {"type": "adaptive"} is the current control surface on Sonnet 5 and Opus 4.8 — there's no separate "thinking effort" knob, effort is the thinking-depth control. Haiku 4.5 isn't in the list of models that accept the effort parameter at all, which is actually fine for your design: extraction is a lookup task, and Anthropic's own guidance is to skip extended thinking for structured extraction, format conversion, and classification — spend nothing on thinking there, spend it on analysis and synthesis instead.

Second structural issue: markdown tables should be your output format, not your working format. Every prompt in both documents says "Markdown Tables ONLY" at the extraction/analysis layer. That's fine for the human-facing final report, but it's the wrong internal representation, for a concrete reason: you have 16 source documents (5 ARs + 6 quarters + 5 decks) that all need to reconcile into one multi-year time series, up to 16 peer companies that need deduplication, and a "Global Source Legend... without duplication" requirement that could run to hundreds of entries by the final report. Parsing your own markdown tables back out to merge them is more failure-prone than never leaving structured data in the first place. Mirror the report_findings shape instead — {claim, value, unit, fiscal_period, source_doc, source_page, source_note, confidence} as JSON — and render markdown only at the very end, deterministically, from validated records. This also directly fixes a real bug: Fin_analysis_prompt1 and Fin_analysis_prompt2 independently produce overlapping red-flag findings (both do CFO/NI reconciliation, DSO analysis, receivables red flags) with no shared context — run as two isolated markdown-table calls today, they can output different confidence scores or slightly different numbers for the same flag, and nothing reconciles that before it reaches the final report. With JSON records keyed consistently, a code-level merge (not an LLM asked to "notice duplicates") does this correctly.

Third: your yfinance plan and your deep-research prompt aren't talking to each other. The docx's "Task 5, 6 & 7" asks the deep-research LLM to find and tabulate price-per-share and market cap for T through T-5 (and possibly T-10/T-12), plus sectoral index returns for the euphoria/panic cycle check. That's exactly the data yfinance gives you exactly, instantly, with zero hallucination risk — running it through a web-search-and-transcribe step is strictly worse on every axis (slower, costlier, and less auditable, since "I found this price on a webpage" is weaker evidence than a timestamped API pull). General rule worth applying everywhere in this pipeline: if a deterministic feed exists (price, index level, market cap, shares outstanding), pull it in code and never ask an LLM to go find it. Reserve deep research for judgment calls — which companies are real peers, what management is signaling, regulatory history — that a script genuinely can't do.

Fourth — resolving "dynamic, not a flow." You want deep research to be able to happen before or after understanding the company's segment mix, depending on the company. That's a real requirement, but "let the orchestrator LLM decide in the moment" is in tension with "traceable" — you want to be able to explain why research happened at step 2 instead of step 5, and an ungrounded in-context judgment call doesn't give you that. The fix is a small, explicit triage step with rule-based (not vibes-based) branch conditions: if the latest AR shows one reportable segment and stable revenue mix, treat it as a pure play and run sector/industry research first, since you already know the industry; if segment count > 1, or the revenue mix shifted materially YoY, or there's a recent M&A/demerger, extract financials and the segment breakdown first, then branch deep research per active segment. This keeps "dynamic" honest — the order varies, but the reason it varied is logged.
