# Sector Playbook — Renewables (manufacturing & generation)

*Tier 2. Family: `commodities_energy` (`prompts/sector_packs/commodities_energy.md`). Shared
rules: `prompts/31`.*
**Provenance:** corpus-grounded — Waaree Energies (Yes Securities, Sep-25; Nuvama, Mar-25; Emkay
Global, Dec-25 — three independent initiations on the same name, the corpus's best triangulation
opportunity), Inox Wind (Axis Capital, Jun-24), Adani Green Energy (JM Financial, Nov-25).

## The economic engine
**Two entirely different businesses share this playbook and must be separated first**, because they
have opposite exposures to the same variable — the module price:

1. **Manufacturing** (modules, cells, wafers, ingots, inverters, wind turbines). A **price-taker on
   a deflationary product**, protected in India by trade and content policy rather than by
   technology. `EBITDA = GW shipped × (realisation per watt − cost per watt)`. Falling module prices
   compress its spread.
2. **Generation / IPP** (solar, wind, hybrid, storage). Sells electricity under long PPAs.
   `Revenue = capacity (MW) × CUF × 8,760 × tariff`. **Falling module prices are a tailwind** — they
   lower the cost of new capacity and raise project returns.

A company doing both is naturally hedged, and a company doing one is exposed. Say which, in the note.

**The manufacturing side's defining fact is that its output price falls structurally.** Solar module
prices have fallen for four decades and Chinese overcapacity keeps them falling. So:

> **Never extrapolate current per-watt spreads.** A manufacturer's margin today reflects a policy-
> protected supply shortage, not a durable cost advantage.

The Indian protection stack is specific, dated, and the whole reason current spreads exist: **ALMM**
(Approved List of Models and Manufacturers, now extending to cells), **DCR** (domestic content
requirement for government-linked demand), basic customs duty on cells and modules, and PLI.
Waaree's thesis rests explicitly on "leading DCR market" position and ALMM compliance. **Every one
of these has a review or expiry date — find it and put it in the note.** Backward integration is the
only defence that survives the protection ending, which is why Waaree's ingot-wafer build
(14 GW planned) matters more than its module capacity.

## Analysis sequence
1. **Classify the business** — manufacturing, generation, EPC, or a mix — with revenue and EBITDA
   split. Waaree carries all three (modules plus EPC through Waaree Renewable Technologies).
2. **For manufacturing: the integration ladder.** Module → cell → wafer → ingot → polysilicon, with
   capacity at each stage and the commissioning date of each. Waaree: 15 GW module (Jun'25) → 28.9 GW
   by FY28E including 3.2 GW US, with cell at 15.4 GW and wafer at 14.0 GW. **The gap between module
   capacity and cell/wafer capacity is the China dependence**, and closing it is the thesis.
3. **Per-watt economics** — realisation per watt, cost per watt, EBITDA per watt, by product and by
   market (domestic DCR / domestic non-DCR / export). Export realisations, especially US, differ
   enough that a blended figure is meaningless.
4. **Order book in GW and its quality** — value, tenure, customer, price-variation clause and
   advance received. Waaree: ~25 GW pending order book at Q1FY26. **Ask whether the order price is
   fixed**: a fixed-price multi-year order book in a deflationary input market is an option the
   customer holds against you, unless module cost falls faster.
5. **For generation: CUF, PPA book and the pipeline.** Capacity operational vs under construction vs
   "vision"; CUF by resource and region; PPA counterparty, tariff and tenure (Adani Green: 81% of
   capacity under 25-year PPAs); and evacuation/transmission connectivity, which is the real
   constraint in India today.
6. **Concentration risk, geographic and customer.** Adani Green's own disclosed risk is 30 GW of a
   50 GW vision in one region (Khavda) — a single-site resource, evacuation and regulatory
   concentration.
7. **The policy stack with dates** — ALMM, DCR, BCD, PLI, ISTS-charge waiver expiry. Then ask what
   the economics look like on the far side of each.
8. **Working capital and funding.** Manufacturing needs advances and inventory; generation needs
   project debt. Both are capital-hungry and both dilute — check the equity plan.
9. **Adjacencies, sceptically** — inverters, BESS, electrolysers, green hydrogen. Waaree announces
   3 GW inverters, 3.5 GWh storage, 300 MW electrolysers. Value announced adjacencies at zero until
   there is revenue; they are optionality, not earnings.

## Signature KPIs
| KPI | Formula | Unit | How to read it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** | installed / operational capacity by stage | GW | For manufacturers, per stage — module, cell, wafer, ingot. **The stage gaps are the import dependence.** For IPPs, operational vs under-construction vs "vision" (Adani Green: 16.7 GW Sep'25, 50 GW by 2030 — three different numbers, and only the first is earning) | Decks, AR |
| **Capacity utilisation** | production / installed capacity (manufacturing); CUF = generation / (capacity × 8,760) for IPPs | % | Two different meanings — state which. Manufacturing utilisation drives operating leverage; **CUF drives IPP revenue directly** and varies by resource quality, region and technology | Decks, CEA |
| **Realisation per watt** | segment revenue / watts shipped | INR/W | Against the global module-price index, by market (DCR / non-DCR / export). Structurally falling; a rising realisation is almost always a mix shift toward DCR or US | Computed |
| **Order book** | pending order book | GW | With value, tenure, customer and the price-variation clause. Waaree's ~25 GW is the revenue visibility — but convert it at a historical realisation and check against capacity, or it is just a number | Decks |
| **EBITDA per watt** | segment EBITDA / watts shipped | INR/W | The spread, and the number that must **not** be extrapolated. Show it against the module-price index so the reader sees the policy-protection component | Computed |

## Supporting KPIs
Capacity by stage with commissioning dates; capacity in the US or other export geographies
separately; DCR vs non-DCR vs export revenue mix; cell and wafer sourcing (in-house vs imported vs
Chinese); technology mix (mono PERC / TOPCon / HJT) and the migration capex; module efficiency
(W per panel) and warranty/degradation terms; franchise and retail-channel partners (Waaree: ~388
franchise partners — a genuine differentiator in the rooftop/retail segment); order-book value in
INR and implied realisation; advances from customers; inventory days and inventory price risk (a
deflating input held in stock is a loss); capex per GW by stage; net debt and the equity-raise plan;
warranty provisioning as % of revenue; receivable days.
*For IPPs:* capacity operational / under construction / pipeline; CUF by asset and resource; PPA
counterparty, tariff and tenure; merchant vs contracted share; curtailment hours; evacuation and
ISTS connectivity status; land secured; project IRR and levelised cost of energy; net debt/EBITDA
and DSCR; per-MW capex; run-rate EBITDA per MW (JM's Adani Green valuation runs on "run rate EBITDA
for 28 GW in FY28" — a construction, not a report, and it should be labelled as one).

## Valuation convention
**Manufacturing: EV/EBITDA or P/E on forward earnings, with a PEG cross-check, and the per-watt
spread assumption published.** Waaree (Yes Securities, Sep-25) is the corpus's example: 22x FY28E
with an "implied PEG of 0.5x" — growth-adjusted anchoring, the third-most-rigorous justification in
`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §4. Because the earnings base depends on a policy-protected spread,
**state the module-price and spread assumption the target implies** — the same discipline the NALCO
note applies to LME in `ferrous_non_ferrous_metals`.

**Generation: EV/EBITDA on run-rate EBITDA at a stated capacity, or DCF/per-MW.** Adani Green is
valued at "14x EBITDA FY28 (run rate EBITDA for 28 GW in FY28)" — note the two embedded assumptions,
the multiple *and* the capacity, and that only ~16.7 GW was operational at the time. Say both out
loud; a target resting on unbuilt capacity is a construction-execution bet as much as a valuation.

Where a company is both, **SOTP** with a manufacturing multiple and a generation multiple, plus the
implied blended multiple as a sanity check.

*Traps:* (i) **extrapolating current per-watt spreads** — the single most important trap in this
playbook; (ii) valuing announced GW rather than commissioned GW, for both factories and power
plants; (iii) treating a fixed-price order book as pure upside when input prices are falling — it
can be either; (iv) crediting adjacencies (BESS, electrolysers, hydrogen) with earnings before
revenue; (v) applying a manufacturer's growth multiple to an IPP's annuity, or vice versa;
(vi) ignoring the equity dilution that the capex plan requires; (vii) capitalising a policy-driven
spread past the policy's expiry date.

## Divergence cases

*Same verified fact, two defensible readings, and the evidence that settles them. The rule
and the closed vocabulary of conditioning variables are in `docs/OPINION_VS_ANALYSIS.md`
§7. `prompts/33_thesis_synthesis.md` seeds `state/interpretation_ledger.json` from these;
`prompts/34_thesis_redteam.md` checks 16-18 audit the result. A reading that names no
conditioner is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).*

**1. The manufacturer is valued at 22x FY28E.**
- *High for a manufacturer* (`own_history_anchor`).
- *Growth-adjusted parity* (`growth_rate`) — Waaree (Yes Securities, Sep-25) publishes an
  implied PEG of 0.5x. Growth-adjusted anchoring is the third-most-rigorous of the four
  justification families.
- *Discriminator* (`historical_distribution`) — how often a module manufacturer has held
  that growth rate for the number of years a 0.5x PEG implicitly assumes. And because the
  earnings base depends on a policy-protected spread, the module-price and per-watt spread
  assumption the target implies must be stated — the same discipline the NALCO note applies
  to LME.

**2. Generation is valued at "14x EBITDA FY28 (run-rate EBITDA for 28 GW in FY28)".**
- *A reasonable infrastructure multiple* (`peer_set_choice`).
- *Two embedded assumptions* (`terminal_value_share`) — that the capacity arrives, and that
  the run-rate is achieved on it. Neither is in the multiple; both are in the target.
- *Discriminator* (`forward_observable`) — the commissioning schedule with dates, and the
  PPA-signed share of the pipeline.

## Forensic screens (sector-specific)
- Capacity announced, capacity "commissioned", and capacity *producing at rated yield* used
  interchangeably — three different numbers, and the gap is the story.
- Order book restated, or the same order counted in both the parent and an EPC subsidiary.
- Realisation per watt rising while global module prices fall, without a stated mix shift — check
  whether it is DCR/US mix or a related-party sale.
- Inventory build while module prices fall — an unrecognised loss sitting in current assets.
- Cell/wafer "capacity" that is actually a signed MoU or a land allotment.
- Warranty and performance-guarantee provisions unchanged as volumes multiply (modules carry
  25-year performance warranties; under-provisioning here is a long-dated liability).
- Capitalisation of R&D, line-commissioning or trial-production costs.
- Export revenue to a related entity in the US or elsewhere; the US subsidiary's margin not
  separately disclosed.
- PLI or duty-drawback incentive income presented inside operating EBITDA.
- For IPPs: capacity "under implementation" counted in the EBITDA base; curtailment losses not
  disclosed; receivables from state discoms ageing (see `power_utilities`); SPV-level debt and
  guarantees outside the headline net-debt figure; asset-recycling gains (InvIT/stake sales)
  presented as operating cash flow.
- Group-level concentration where a promoter entity is also the customer or the EPC contractor.

## Dependencies to map
**The policy stack, each with its date:** ALMM (and its extension to cells), DCR requirements, basic
customs duty on cells/modules, PLI tranches, ISTS-charge waiver expiry, RPO trajectory · India's
500 GW non-fossil target by 2030 and the annual auction/tender pipeline (SECI, NTPC, state
utilities) — the demand side · Chinese polysilicon, wafer, cell and module prices and export policy;
US Section 201/232, AD/CVD and UFLPA rules for anyone shipping to the US, plus the IRA's domestic-
content incentives · transmission and evacuation capacity, which is now the binding constraint on
Indian RE build-out · land acquisition · discom PPA-signing behaviour and payment record (link to
`power_utilities`) · interest rates and project-debt availability · storage economics and
battery-cell prices, which determine whether "firm and dispatchable" RE tenders are viable ·
wind-resource assessment and turbine technology cycles for wind names (Inox Wind).

## Common archetypes here
`capex-to-cashflow` (capacity commissioning — the dominant archetype for both halves),
`regulatory-tailwind` (ALMM/DCR/PLI — legitimate, but the tailwind has a dated end, and saying so is
the analysis), `margin-expansion` via backward integration (the only durable margin story here),
`market-share-gainer`, and `cyclical-peak`, which deserves unusual prominence: **a policy-protected
per-watt spread in a structurally deflationary product is close to a textbook cyclical peak dressed
as structural growth.** `re-rating` and `quality-compounder` claims in module manufacturing should
be treated with the highest skepticism weight until backward integration is commissioned and
operating.
