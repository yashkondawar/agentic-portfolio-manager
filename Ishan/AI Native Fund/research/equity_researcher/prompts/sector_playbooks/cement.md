# Sector Playbook — Cement

*Tier 2. Family: `commodities_energy` (`prompts/sector_packs/commodities_energy.md`). Shared

**Provenance:** corpus-grounded — Nuvoco Vistas (BOB Capital, Sep-25, 25pp, 29 exhibits — a rare
HOLD initiation and the corpus's cleanest per-tonne bridge), Dalmia Bharat (Nirmal Bang, Mar-23),
a cement-pricing sector note (Nirmal Bang, Mar-16), plus the tile/building-materials adjacency
(Kajaria — HDFC Securities Jan-21, Nirmal Bang Sep-18 and Mar-24 industry).

## The economic engine
Cement is the purest per-tonne spread business in the registry, and the **only one where freight
makes the market local rather than global**:

`EBITDA = volume × (realisation per tonne − cost per tonne)`

Nuvoco's forecast table is the model presentation — four rows, and the whole thesis: volumes
19.4 → 23.9 mt, realisations INR 4,858 → 5,017/t, operating cost INR 4,629 → 4,539/t, and
**EBITDA/t 707 → 940** across FY25-FY28E. Note what that arithmetic says: realisation rises 3%
while EBITDA/t rises 33%, so **the entire story is cost, not price** — and the note's own exhibit
says exactly that ("EBITDA margin improvement purely on cost"). Always run this bridge; it
usually reveals that a cement thesis attributed to pricing is actually a fuel-cost thesis.

Two structural features distinguish cement from metals:

- **The market is regional, and freight is why.** Cement cannot travel far economically, so a
  national average realisation conceals everything that matters. Nuvoco's realisation profile is
  a mix statement: eastern exposure reduced to 61% with more weight in "better realisation"
  regions. Analyse by region, with lead distances.
- **Capacity is the industry's discipline problem.** Utilisation, not demand, sets pricing power.
  Nuvoco held ~77% utilisation FY20-25 and is expected at ~80% FY25-28E — the level at which
  regional pricing discipline becomes possible.

## Analysis sequence
1. **Map capacity physically**: clinker capacity vs grinding capacity, by plant, by region, plus
   split/standalone grinding units and their feedstock source. Clinker is the capital-intensive,
   permission-heavy step; grinding is cheap and fast. The two numbers are different and both matter.
2. **Region-by-region volume, realisation and market share** — with the company's lead distance in
   each. A regional realisation series is the honest one.
3. **Build the four-row per-tonne bridge** (volume, realisation, cost, EBITDA/t) for as many years
   as available, then decompose the cost line: fuel/power, freight, raw material, employee, other.
4. **The fuel equation.** Pet coke vs imported/domestic coal vs alternative fuels (AFR), the
   thermal substitution rate, and the landed cost per kcal. This is the largest swing item in the
   cost stack and the most common cause of a "margin expansion" that is really a fuel cycle.
5. **Blending and the clinker factor.** Higher blended-cement share (PPC/PSC/composite) means less
   clinker per tonne of cement — lower cost, lower carbon, and more effective grinding capacity.
   Nuvoco's cement-to-clinker ratio at ~1.7x, down from 1.8x, is a *cost and capacity* statement.
   **Watch the convention**: some notes quote clinker factor (clinker/cement, <1), others
   cement-to-clinker (>1). State which you are using — they move in opposite directions.
6. **Freight and logistics** — lead distance, road/rail mix, direct-dispatch share, and the
   depot network. Freight is often the second-largest cost line and is structural, not cyclical.
7. **Power** — captive/WHRS/solar share of power consumed. Waste-heat recovery is the sector's
   highest-return capex and a permanent cost advantage once built.
8. **Regional supply pipeline** — capacity additions announced in each of the company's markets
   over three years, against demand growth. This, not national data, sets pricing.
9. **Premium-product and trade/non-trade mix.** Trade (retail, bagged) realisation exceeds
   non-trade (institutional, bulk) materially; a mix shift moves realisation without any price
   change.
10. **Then mid-cycle EBITDA/t, balance sheet, and the multiple.**

## Signature KPIs
| KPI | Formula | Unit | How to read it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EBITDA per tonne** | EBITDA / tonnes sold | INR/t | The sector's single number. **Bridge every change into realisation and each cost line** — Nuvoco's 707 → 940 is a cost story with a 3% realisation contribution, and saying so is the analysis | Computed from volume + EBITDA |
| **Realisation per tonne** | revenue / tonnes sold | INR/t | **By region, never blended nationally.** Then split price from mix (trade/non-trade, premium products, geography) — a mix-driven realisation rise is not pricing power | Computed |
| **Capacity utilisation** | production / installed capacity | % | The industry's pricing-discipline gauge and the company's operating leverage. ~75-80% is where regional discipline becomes possible (Nuvoco ~77% FY20-25 → ~80% FY25-28E) | AR, decks |
| **Lead distance** | average distance from plant to market | km | The freight cost driver and the reason the market is regional. Rising lead distance means the company is selling further from home — chasing volume at a worse netback | Decks, transcripts |
| **Clinker factor** | clinker consumed / cement produced | x | The blending measure — lower clinker factor means lower cost, lower carbon and more usable grinding capacity. Nuvoco's inverse (cement-to-clinker ~1.7x, from 1.8x). **State the convention** | AR, decks |

## Supporting KPIs
Clinker capacity vs grinding capacity by plant and region; volume by region; regional market
share; trade vs non-trade mix; premium-product share; blended-cement (PPC/PSC/composite) share;
fuel mix (pet coke / imported coal / domestic coal / AFR) and thermal substitution rate; fuel cost
per kcal and per tonne of cement; power consumed per tonne (kWh/t) and the captive/WHRS/solar
share; freight cost per tonne with road/rail split; direct-dispatch share; raw-material cost per
tonne and limestone reserve life; employee cost per tonne; capex per tonne of new capacity and
commissioning dates; net debt and net debt/EBITDA (Nuvoco's own EV/tonne band is explicitly
described as weighed down by balance-sheet concerns — leverage caps the multiple in this sector);
ROCE; working-capital days; CO2 per tonne of cementitious material and the decarbonisation capex
ahead; EV per tonne of capacity in US$ (Nuvoco: US$74-82 FY26-28E).

## Standard exhibit set
The four-row per-tonne bridge (volume, realisation, cost, EBITDA/t) across all available years ·
cost stack per tonne decomposed by line · fuel mix and fuel cost per kcal against pet-coke and
coal indices · EBITDA/t against the regional realisation index · realisation by region with lead
distance annotated · regional volume and market share · trade vs non-trade and premium mix ·
capacity by plant split clinker vs grinding · utilisation trend vs the regional average ·
clinker factor / cement-to-clinker ratio trend · regional capacity-addition pipeline against
regional demand growth · WHRS and renewable share of power · freight cost per tonne with rail
share · net debt/EBITDA · capex pipeline with capex per tonne · **EV/tonne band and EV/EBITDA
one-year-forward band** (Nuvoco carries both, and the pair is the sector convention) · peer table
on EBITDA/t, EV/tonne, utilisation and leverage — not on multiples alone.

## Valuation convention
**EV per tonne of capacity and EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle EBITDA, side by side.** The pair is the
sector's standard because each covers the other's blind spot: EV/tonne prices the asset base
against replacement cost and transaction comparables and is insensitive to where the cycle sits;
EV/EBITDA prices current earnings power. Nuvoco is published on both — EV/EBITDA 6.4x/6.1x/5.9x
and EV/tonne US$74/81/82 for FY26/27/28E — and rated **HOLD**, which is worth noting because only
1% of the corpus's initiations are HOLD (`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §5). The corpus's own
positive-rating bias makes a defensible non-BUY unusually informative.

**Regional pricing means national averages mislead**, so anchor the multiple against peers with
comparable regional exposure, not against the national peer set. A South-heavy and a North-heavy
producer at the same EV/EBITDA are not equivalently priced.

*Traps:* (i) capitalising a fuel-cost trough as structural margin — the sector's most common error
and the one the four-row bridge exposes; (ii) peak-on-peak in a strong pricing year;
(iii) crediting announced grinding capacity as if it were clinker capacity;
(iv) EV/tonne compared across regions without adjusting for realisation and freight;
(v) ignoring leverage, which in this sector directly compresses the multiple regardless of
operating performance; (vi) valuing consolidated realisation when the mix is shifting regions.

## Divergence cases

*Same verified fact, two defensible readings, and the evidence that settles them. The rule
and the closed vocabulary of conditioning variables are in `docs/OPINION_VS_ANALYSIS.md`
§7. `prompts/33_thesis_synthesis.md` seeds `state/interpretation_ledger.json` from these;
`prompts/34_thesis_redteam.md` checks 16-18 audit the result. A reading that names no
conditioner is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).*

**1. EV per tonne is US$74 against a replacement cost of roughly US$100-120.**
- *Cheap on assets* (`capital_intensity`) — below the cost of building it.
- *The question is unanswered* (`cycle_position`) — EV/tonne is deliberately insensitive to
  where the cycle sits, so it cannot tell you whether current earnings power supports the
  price. Nuvoco is published on both (EV/EBITDA 6.4x/6.1x/5.9x and EV/tonne US$74/81/82 for
  FY26/27/28E) and rated HOLD — a defensible non-BUY, which is unusually informative given
  that only 1% of the corpus's initiations are HOLD.
- *Discriminator* (`peer_distribution`) — the pair, side by side. Each covers the other's
  blind spot; that is why the sector's standard is both.

**2. Realisation rose 6%.**
- *Pricing power* (`growth_durability`).
- *A regional print* (`peer_set_choice`) — national averages mislead in a sector where
  pricing is set within 300km. A 6% national average can be 12% in one region and flat in
  another, and the company's regional mix decides which it experienced.
- *Discriminator* (`disclosed_mechanism`) — region-wise price data set against the company's
  own regional capacity mix.

## Forensic screens (sector-specific)
- **EBITDA/t improvement wholly attributable to fuel cost** but narrated as pricing or
  premiumisation — run the bridge and check which line moved.
- Realisation rising only through mix (region, trade share, premium products) while like-for-like
  regional price is flat.
- Volume growth achieved by extending lead distance — netback per tonne is falling even as
  reported realisation holds.
- Utilisation computed on clinker capacity in one year and grinding capacity in another, or on
  rated vs effective capacity.
- Clinker factor / cement-to-clinker convention switched between periods, changing the apparent
  direction.
- Capitalisation of mine-development, overburden-removal or plant trial-run costs; capex
  reclassified from maintenance to growth to flatter free cash flow.
- Limestone lease renewals or mining-clearance expiries inside the forecast horizon, undisclosed.
- Inventory build (clinker or cement) in a soft-pricing quarter, supporting reported production.
- Freight cost moved between "other expenses" and netted against revenue (FOR vs ex-works
  contracting) — this changes both realisation and cost per tonne on identical economics.
- Related-party logistics, limestone or fly-ash/slag supply arrangements.
- Non-cement businesses (RMC, aggregates, building materials) blended into the per-tonne maths.
- Emission and pollution-norm capex deferred; carbon-compliance liability not provided.

## Dependencies to map
Regional cement demand — government infrastructure capex, housing starts, rural demand — by state,
because the market is regional · pet coke and coal prices, plus pet-coke import policy and any
usage restriction · diesel and rail freight tariffs; wagon availability · limestone lease auctions,
mining and forest clearances · state-level royalty and cess · electricity tariffs and open-access
rules for captive/renewable power · CO2 norms, the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme and CBAM for
exporters · regional capacity-addition announcements by every competitor (the single most
important research deliverable in this sector) · industry consolidation and the pricing discipline
of the two largest groups · fly ash and slag availability, which caps blending.

## Common archetypes here
`cyclical-recovery` and `cyclical-peak` (decide which, using regional utilisation and the
supply pipeline), `margin-expansion` (test hard against the fuel cycle — this is where condition 5
of `prompts/thesis_archetypes/margin-expansion.md` earns its place), `capex-to-cashflow`
(commissioning of announced capacity), `balance-sheet-repair` (leverage is the binding
constraint on the multiple for the mid-cap names), `market-share-gainer` (must be regional to
mean anything), and `special-situation` for the sector's frequent consolidation. Watch for
`quality-compounder` claims: durable outperformance here requires a permanent freight or fuel
advantage, and those are visible in the cost stack or they do not exist.
