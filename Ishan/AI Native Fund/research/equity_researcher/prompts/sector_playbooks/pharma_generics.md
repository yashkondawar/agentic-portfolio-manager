# Sector Playbook — Generics, APIs & domestic formulations

*Tier 2. Family: `pharma_chemicals` (`prompts/sector_packs/pharma_chemicals.md`). Shared rules:
`prompts/31`.*
**Provenance:** corpus-grounded — Granules India (Elara Capital, Mar-26 — the corpus's cleanest
worked case of a warning letter gating the margin forecast), Supriya Lifescience (Choice Broking,
Nov-25 — API, 38+ commercial molecules, the demand-pull-vs-capacity-led distinction), Piramal Pharma
(JM Financial, Dec-24) for the integrated generics-plus-CDMO structure, Zydus Wellness (BOB Capital,
Mar-22) for the consumer-health adjacency.

## The economic engine
A generic drug is a **chemically identical copy sold into a market where the only differentiators are
price, regulatory access and reliability of supply.** So the economics are a race:

`Revenue per molecule = market size × share × price` — where **price falls, always**

US generic price erosion runs mid-to-high single digits annually as a structural baseline, worse when
a molecule attracts new entrants. **The business is therefore not a growth business at the molecule
level; it is a portfolio-replenishment business.** Value accrues to whoever can:

1. **File and launch faster** — the ANDA pipeline, Para IV challenges, first-to-file exclusivity.
2. **Make it cheaper** — backward integration into the API, process chemistry, scale.
3. **Stay compliant** — because losing a facility loses every molecule made in it.

**Complexity is the only escape from erosion, and it is the axis the whole sector is climbing.**
Granules' thesis states it directly: complex generics and differentiated formulations are "less
exposed to the price erosion dynamics of US generics and can command structurally higher margins",
and as their share rises "gross margins should structurally improve." **Establish where on the
simple→complex spectrum the revenue actually sits**, not where the strategy slide says it is going.

**Compliance is a binary, not a discount rate.** This is the feature that distinguishes this playbook
from every other manufacturing sector in the registry. A USFDA warning letter or import alert stops
approvals and shipments from that site. Granules' entire margin forecast — EBITDA margin 21.6% (FY26E)
to 22.5% (FY28E) — is explicitly conditioned on **resolution of the USFDA warning letter**, and two
of the note's exhibits say so in their titles. Copy that treatment: **if a site is under action, the
margin forecast is a compliance forecast, and the note must say so on page one.**

## Analysis sequence
1. **Segment revenue by geography and business** — US, EU, emerging markets, domestic formulations,
   API/CRAMS, consumer health. These have entirely different pricing mechanics: US is erosive and
   tender-driven, domestic India is brand-led and price-controlled, APIs are spread businesses.
   Granules at 77% US revenue contribution is a concentration statement that frames everything.
2. **Facility-by-facility compliance status.** Every manufacturing site, its regulator
   classifications (VAI/OAI), inspection dates, open observations, warning letters and import alerts —
   **and the revenue attributable to each site.** Quantify revenue at risk per site. Then assess
   remediation credibility against the company's *own* past record, not against management intent.
3. **The US portfolio and pipeline** — commercialised ANDAs, pending ANDAs, Para IV filings,
   first-to-file/exclusivity opportunities, and the competitive intensity per molecule (how many
   approved players). A pending-ANDA count without therapeutic mix and competitor count is a vanity
   metric.
4. **Price erosion, measured.** Like-for-like realisation change on the existing basket, separated
   from new-launch contribution. **This is the number companies most avoid disclosing**; derive it
   where possible (US revenue growth minus new-launch contribution minus volume) and label the
   derivation.
5. **Complexity mix** — oral solids vs injectables/peptides/inhalation/transdermal/complex
   formulations, as a share of revenue and of gross profit. Then the gross-margin differential
   between them.
6. **Backward integration** — API self-sufficiency for key molecules, KSM/intermediate sourcing and
   the China dependence on the rest. This is the cost-curve position, and the sector's China exposure
   is on the input side even for exporters.
7. **Domestic formulations, if material** — therapy mix, the NLEM/price-control exposure, MR
   (medical-representative) productivity, field-force size, and the top-brand concentration.
   IQVIA/AWACS secondary-sales data is the check on primary sales.
8. **R&D intensity and its composition** — total R&D as % of sales, split between generic filings and
   complex/novel development, plus the capitalisation policy.
9. **Working capital and capacity** — inventory (long shelf-life chains run heavy), receivables from
   US distributors and consolidated buying groups, capacity utilisation. Supriya's framing is worth
   borrowing: 85-86% historic utilisation with a new facility easing it to ~75% is "strategic slack
   created ahead of visible demand" — a *demand-pull* expansion, not a capacity-led growth story.
   Making that distinction explicitly is good practice.
10. **Then the multiple, differentiated by complexity and compliance** (see convention below).

## Signature KPIs

## Supporting KPIs
Revenue by geography (US / EU / EM / India) and by business (formulations / API / CRAMS / consumer);
complex vs plain-vanilla revenue share; injectables and other complex-dosage share; new-launch
contribution to US revenue; like-for-like price erosion on the base basket; commercialised ANDA count
and approvals received per year; DMF filings; API self-sufficiency % for key molecules; KSM/intermediate
China dependence; capacity by site and utilisation (Supriya: 85-86% historic, ~75% post-commissioning);
capex per unit of capacity and commissioning dates; doses/units manufactured (Granules: ~10bn doses);
domestic therapy mix, NLEM-covered share, field force and revenue per MR, top-10 brand concentration;
customer concentration (US buying groups are three consortia — concentration is structural);
receivable days by geography; inventory days and slow-moving/expiry provisioning; remediation capex
and consultant cost; litigation provisions (Para IV, price-fixing, opioid-type exposures);
gross-to-net adjustments (chargebacks, rebates, shelf-stock adjustments, failure-to-supply penalties);
ROCE and ROCE excluding under-construction assets; "core" vs reported EPS and what is adjusted out.

## Standard exhibit set
Revenue by geography and business with each one's pricing regime named · **facility compliance table:
site, regulator, last inspection, classification, open observations, attributable revenue** ·
complex vs plain revenue mix over time with gross margin by bucket · gross margin against key input
indices (to test the mix-vs-cycle question) · US revenue decomposed into base erosion plus new
launches · like-for-like price erosion series · ANDA pipeline by therapy, complexity and competitor
count, with Para IV/FTF flagged · approvals received and launches per year · API self-sufficiency
and China dependence by key molecule · capacity and utilisation by site with the expansion pipeline ·
R&D as % of sales split by purpose, with capitalisation shown · domestic therapy mix and NLEM
exposure · field-force productivity · gross-to-net reconciliation · working-capital days by
geography · litigation and contingent-liability schedule · ROCE trend · P/E and EV/EBITDA bands ·
peer table **segmented by complexity and compliance status**, not by size.

## Valuation convention
**P/E or EV/EBITDA on forward earnings — but the sector's cardinal rule is that a clean-facility
complex-generics or CDMO business and a commodity generic must not share a multiple.** The peer table
must be segmented by complexity and compliance, or it is misleading by construction.

Granules is stated as **16.7x FY28E core EPS** (Elara) — and note two things about that formulation
worth replicating: the base is rolled two years forward from the note date (so per
`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §4, state the base year and show the un-rolled target), and it is **core**
EPS, i.e. adjusted. **Always publish the adjustment list**; "core" in this sector routinely excludes
remediation costs, one-off litigation and impairments, several of which recur.

Where compliance is unresolved, the honest structure is **two scenarios with the multiple attached to
each** — resolution and non-resolution — rather than a single point estimate silently assuming
resolution. Where the company is a genuine blend, use **SOTP**: a CDMO/complex leg at its own
peer-anchored multiple and the commodity leg at another, publishing the implied blended multiple as a
sanity check (the pattern in `specialty_chemicals`).

*Traps:* (i) **applying a complex/CDMO multiple to commodity generic earnings** — the family's most
common error, named in the pack; (ii) forecasting margin recovery that is really a compliance
assumption, without saying so; (iii) capitalising a one-off exclusivity or limited-competition
windfall (a gAlbuterol-type shortage) as run-rate; (iv) accepting "core" EPS without the adjustment
list; (v) valuing a pending-ANDA count as pipeline value without competitor counts; (vi) ignoring
gross-to-net and failure-to-supply exposures in the US; (vii) treating high US share as quality.

## Divergence cases

*Same verified fact, two defensible readings, and the evidence that settles them. The rule
and the closed vocabulary of conditioning variables are in `docs/OPINION_VS_ANALYSIS.md`
§7. `prompts/33_thesis_synthesis.md` seeds `state/interpretation_ledger.json` from these;
`prompts/34_thesis_redteam.md` checks 16-18 audit the result. A reading that names no
conditioner is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).*

**1. It trades at 16.7x FY28E core EPS.**
- *Cheap for the sector* (`peer_set_choice`) — Granules is stated this way (Elara).
- *Two qualifications before that number means anything* (`earnings_base_quality`) — the
  base is rolled two years forward from the note date, so state the base year and show the
  un-rolled target; and "core" in this sector routinely excludes remediation costs, one-off
  litigation and impairments, several of which recur. **Always publish the adjustment list.**
- *Discriminator* (`historical_distribution`) — how often those "one-offs" have appeared in
  the last eight years. A cost that recurs every third year is an operating cost.

**2. The peer table is labelled "pharma".**
- *A sector comparison* (`peer_set_choice`).
- *Misleading by construction* (`peer_set_choice`) — a clean-facility complex-generics
  business and a commodity generic must not share a multiple. The cardinal rule of the
  sector.
- *Discriminator* (`peer_distribution`) — the table segmented by product complexity and
  USFDA compliance status.

**3. US revenue grew 30%.**
- *Franchise strength* (`growth_durability`).
- *One launch* (`growth_durability`) — a limited-competition window or a competitor's
  shortage, both of which close on a schedule.
- *Discriminator* (`disclosed_mechanism`) — product-level revenue concentration and the
  expected competitor-entry dates.

## Forensic screens (sector-specific)
- **Margin improvement forecast on an unresolved compliance issue**, without the conditionality stated
  (Granules' note does state it — the absence of such a statement is the flag).
- Warning-letter or import-alert revenue exposure not quantified per site; a site's production
  quietly transferred to another site without disclosure of the approval requirement.
- Price erosion not disclosed, or US growth presented without splitting base erosion from new launches.
- Gross-margin expansion coinciding with a falling KSM/solvent index rather than with complexity mix
  (see `margin-expansion.md` condition 5).
- **Inventory rising ahead of a launch that has not been approved**, or slow-moving/near-expiry stock
  under-provided — this sector's inventory has a hard expiry and provisioning is discretionary.
- Gross-to-net accruals (chargebacks, rebates, shelf-stock adjustments) reduced to support revenue;
  the reconciliation not disclosed.
- R&D capitalised rather than expensed, especially where filings are the output; capitalised
  development written off in a single "exceptional" year.
- Remediation and consultant costs classified as exceptional every year for several years.
- Domestic primary sales (to stockists) outrunning IQVIA/AWACS secondary sales — channel stuffing,
  identical in mechanism to the FMCG screen.
- NLEM/DPCO price-control exposure understated; overcharging liability and interest not provided.
- Related-party API sourcing or loan-licence manufacturing; promoter-owned CRO/CDMO arrangements.
- Litigation: Para IV at-risk launches without provision; US price-fixing and opioid-type exposures
  disclosed only as contingent.
- Subsidiary losses (US front-end, acquired EU generics) parked outside the reported segment.
- Frequent changes to the "core"/"adjusted" earnings definition.

## Dependencies to map
**USFDA** — inspection cadence, the classification database (VAI/OAI), warning letters, import alerts,
and the OGD approval backlog; plus EDQM, MHRA, ANVISA, WHO-GMP and the domestic CDSCO · US generic
price-erosion trend and the three buying consortia's tendering behaviour · Para IV litigation
outcomes and patent expiry calendars (the launch pipeline's timing) · **China** — KSM and intermediate
supply, pricing and export policy, plus environmental shutdowns, which is the input-side dependency
even for exporters · PLI for APIs/KSMs and its localisation targets · NPPA/NLEM revisions and DPCO
ceiling prices for domestic formulations · IQVIA/AWACS secondary-sales data · USD-INR (dollar revenue,
rupee cost) · US IRA drug-pricing provisions and biosimilar/complex-generic policy · EU tender
regimes · anti-dumping duties on APIs and intermediates, with expiry dates · state pollution-control
boards for API manufacture (link to `specialty_chemicals`) · trade policy and the tariff exposure of
US-bound shipments.

## Common archetypes here
`turnaround` (compliance remediation and margin recovery — the sector's most common archetype, and the
one whose thesis is really a regulatory forecast), `margin-expansion` (complexity mix and backward
integration — defensible when the mix is actually moving), `capex-to-cashflow` (capacity and
complex-dosage facilities commissioning), `regulatory-tailwind` (PLI, China+1, patent cliffs) and its
inverse, `special-situation` (demergers separating CDMO from generics — a recurring structure in this
sector), and `re-rating`, which here usually means "should be valued as a CDMO/complex player rather
than a commodity generic" — a legitimate argument **only** when the revenue mix has already shifted,
and otherwise exactly the circular reasoning `prompts/34` is built to catch. Treat
`quality-compounder` claims cautiously: a portfolio facing structural price erosion compounds only if
replenishment outruns it, and that must be shown molecule by molecule.
