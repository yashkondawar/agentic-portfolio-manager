# Sector Playbook — IT services, ER&D & BPM

`prompts/31`.*

the explicit seven-lever margin bridge), Happiest Minds (Axis Capital, Jan-24), KPIT Technologies
(Sushil Finance, Oct-22 — automotive ER&D), MPS (Sushil Finance, Jun-25), a technology sector note
(Choice Broking, Jul-25), Zaggle (Equirus, Apr-24) for the platform-adjacent model.

## The economic engine
An IT services company **rents out people at a markup and keeps the difference between what the
client pays for an outcome and what it costs to staff it.** Every margin lever is a term in that
sentence:

`Revenue = billable headcount × utilisation × realisation per hour`
`Gross margin = 1 − (offshore cost + onsite cost) / revenue`

The Coforge note lists the levers in the order they actually operate, and it is worth reproducing as
a checklist: **offshore mix shift, utilisation improvement, pyramid rationalisation, operating
efficiencies, and currency.** Note what is *not* on that list — pricing. Realisation per hour is
nearly flat in this industry across cycles, so:

> **Margin in IT services comes from cost architecture, not from price. Growth comes from deal wins.
> These are two separate arguments and a note must make both.**

Three structural facts distinguish this playbook from `internet_platforms`:

- **The business scales linearly unless something breaks the link.** Revenue per employee is the
  measure of whether it has. A company growing revenue and headcount at the same rate has no
  operating leverage, whatever the margin commentary claims.
- **Currency is not a rounding error.** Revenue is largely USD/EUR/GBP against an INR cost base, so
  reported INR growth blends operating performance with FX. **Constant-currency growth is the only
  honest series**; report reported-INR, USD and cc growth separately.
- **AI is the live existential question and the company's own narrative is least reliable here.**
  Two directions, both requiring evidence: does AI compress pricing on core application-maintenance
  and testing work (clients now routinely demand the productivity gain as a discount), and does it
  break the headcount-revenue link in the company's favour? The evidence is in deal terms, disclosed
  pricing, and the revenue-per-employee trajectory — not in the number of "AI-led" slides.

## Analysis sequence
1. **Growth, decomposed properly** — constant-currency, then USD, then reported INR, with the FX and
   inorganic contributions separated out. Then by service line, vertical and geography.
2. **Deal momentum, not just the current quarter.** TCV and ACV signed, the large-deal count and
   size, the book-to-bill, and the **renewal vs new-logo split**. A TCV number without duration is
   uninterpretable — a $500mn 7-year deal and a $500mn 2-year deal are different businesses.
3. **Client concentration and mining.** Top-5 and top-10 client share, the count of $1mn / $5mn /
   $10mn / $50mn accounts and its movement between buckets. Account *mining* (clients moving up the
   buckets) is the highest-quality growth in this sector because it costs least to acquire.
4. **Vertical and geography exposure against each end-market's own capex cycle** — BFSI,
   healthcare/life-sciences, retail, manufacturing, hi-tech, travel. Coforge's own risk disclosure
   names "delay in travel segment recovery": vertical exposure is thesis-level, not descriptive.
5. **The margin bridge, using the sector's own levers**: offshore/onshore mix, utilisation
   (excluding trainees — insist on the definition), pyramid (fresher share and average experience),
   sub-contractor cost as % of revenue, SG&A leverage, and currency. Quantify each; an unbridged
   margin forecast is not a forecast.
6. **The people engine** — headcount (billable vs support), gross and net additions, fresher
   hiring, LTM attrition, and offshore-onshore split. Attrition is both a cost driver and a demand
   signal: Coforge's rise from ~10.5% to 16.3% (Q3FY22) tracked the industry-wide demand surge, and
   read that way it is information about the market, not just about the company.
7. **Revenue per employee**, in USD, over five-plus years. This is the single best test of whether
   the business is escaping linearity.
8. **The AI question, with evidence** (see above), plus the delivery-model response: platforms,
   IP/products, managed services, outcome-based pricing share.
9. **Then cash conversion and the multiple.** DSO/DPO, CFO/EBITDA, and the payout — these are
   asset-light businesses and cash return is part of the equity story.

## Signature KPIs
| KPI | Formula | Unit | How to read it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Constant-currency (CC) revenue growth** | revenue growth with FX held at base rates | % | **The only honest growth series.** Report cc, USD and reported-INR side by side, and separate inorganic contribution. A note that leads with reported INR growth in a weak-rupee year is flattering itself | Company factsheet |
| **EBIT margin** | EBIT / revenue | % | Bridge every change into the sector's own levers — offshore mix, utilisation, pyramid, sub-contracting, SG&A, currency. Coforge's disclosed lever list is the template | P&L |
| **Utilisation** | billed hours / available hours | % | **Get the definition** — with or without trainees changes the number by several points. Near the ceiling (~85%) it stops being a lever and becomes a hiring signal | Company factsheet |
| **Attrition (LTM)** | voluntary exits over 12 months / average headcount | % | Use LTM, not quarterly annualised, which is volatile and gameable. Read it as both a cost driver and a demand indicator (Coforge: 10.5% → 16.3% through the FY22 demand surge) | Factsheet |
| **Revenue per employee** | revenue / average headcount | US$ | **The linearity test.** Flat revenue/employee across years means growth is pure headcount and there is no operating leverage to pay a premium for. This is where an AI productivity claim either shows up or does not | Computed |
| **TCV** | total contract value signed in the period | US$ mn | **Always with duration and the renewal/new split.** Book-to-bill (TCV / revenue) is the forward indicator; a TCV figure without tenure is not comparable across periods or peers | Company disclosure |

## Supporting KPIs
ACV and book-to-bill; large-deal count and average size; net-new vs renewal TCV; client count by
revenue bucket ($1mn/$5mn/$10mn/$50mn+) with bucket migration; top-5 and top-10 client
concentration; revenue by vertical and by geography; digital/next-gen revenue share (and its
definition, which companies change); fixed-price vs time-and-materials mix; offshore revenue and
effort mix; onsite/offshore headcount; billable vs support headcount; gross and net headcount adds;
fresher hiring and fresher share of headcount; average experience/pyramid shape; sub-contractor
cost as % of revenue; SG&A as % of revenue; realisation per hour where disclosed; days sales
outstanding and unbilled revenue; CFO/EBITDA; ETR; hedge book and hedging policy with the average
realised rate; visa dependence and localisation %; M&A contribution and the acquired entities'
margins; IP/platform revenue share; ESOP cost as % of revenue.

## Standard exhibit set
Revenue growth in cc, USD and reported INR as three series with inorganic separated · growth by
service line, vertical and geography · TCV and book-to-bill by quarter with large-deal count ·
client-bucket migration table (the exhibit that shows account mining) · top-10 concentration trend ·
**the EBIT margin bridge, lever by lever** (offshore mix, utilisation, pyramid, sub-contracting,
SG&A, currency) · utilisation and attrition (LTM) on one chart · headcount with gross/net adds and
fresher share · **revenue per employee in USD over 5-10 years** (the linearity exhibit) ·
offshore effort mix · sub-contractor cost ratio · vertical exposure against each end-market's capex
cycle · DSO and CFO/EBITDA · hedge book and realised rate · P/E one-year-forward band against the
company's own history · valuation vs tier-1 and tier-2 peers on P/E against cc growth and margin.

## Valuation convention
**Target P/E × forward EPS, anchored against the company's own historical band and its tier peer
set.** Coforge is stated exactly this way — "the stock trades at a valuation (27x FY2024E EPS). This
is justified given strong execution, robust earnings visibility and strong deal wins" — and that
sentence is worth dissecting because it is the sector's standard argument in both its strength and
its weakness: the multiple is defended by *deal visibility*, which is checkable (TCV, book-to-bill),
but "strong execution" is not.

**The growth-margin trade-off gates the multiple.** A tier-2 company growing cc revenue at 15% with
a flat EBIT margin and a tier-2 growing at 12% with 200bps of margin expansion can deserve the same
multiple; the note must say which combination it is paying for. State the peer set explicitly and
justify the tier — tier-1 and tier-2 multiples are structurally different because of scale,
client-mining capacity and balance-sheet optionality, not because of quality alone.

*Traps:* (i) paying a premium for reported-INR growth that is FX; (ii) capitalising a demand-surge
year's growth (FY22 is the cautionary case — the same year attrition peaked); (iii) applying a
tier-1 multiple to a tier-2 without the client-mining and scale evidence; (iv) crediting a margin
forecast whose bridge does not add up, or that assumes utilisation above the practical ceiling;
(v) valuing acquired revenue at organic multiples without the acquired margins and integration
record; (vi) accepting an AI-productivity narrative that is not visible in revenue per employee.

## Divergence cases

*Same verified fact, two defensible readings, and the evidence that settles them. The rule
and the closed vocabulary of conditioning variables are in `docs/OPINION_VS_ANALYSIS.md`
§7. `prompts/33_thesis_synthesis.md` seeds `state/interpretation_ledger.json` from these;
`prompts/34_thesis_redteam.md` checks 16-18 audit the result. A reading that names no
conditioner is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).*

**1. It trades at 27x FY24E EPS, "justified given strong execution, robust earnings
visibility and strong deal wins".**
- *Justified* (`growth_durability`) — that is Coforge's actual framing, and it is the
  sector's standard argument.
- *Half of it is checkable and half is not* (`growth_durability`) — deal visibility is
  measurable (TCV, book-to-bill); "strong execution" is an unearned adjective (§2 F6).
  Split the sentence and keep only the half with a number under it.
- *Discriminator* (`disclosed_mechanism`) — TCV, book-to-bill, and the historical
  deal-to-revenue conversion lag.

**2. Two tier-2 companies trade at the same P/E.**
- *One of them is mispriced* (`peer_set_choice`).
- *Both can be right* (`growth_rate` versus `incremental_roce`) — a tier-2 growing cc
  revenue at 15% with a flat EBIT margin and a tier-2 growing at 12% with 200bps of margin
  expansion can deserve the same multiple. **The note must say which combination it is
  paying for.** The growth-margin trade-off gates the multiple.
- *Discriminator* (`peer_distribution`) — decompose forward EPS growth into revenue growth
  and margin change for both, and state which leg the target multiple is buying.

**3. 27x against a ten-year median of 19x.**
- *Expensive on its own history* (`own_history_anchor`).
- *The band is not comparable* (`own_history_anchor`) — the historical band was set at a
  different tier position, service mix and offshore leverage. A multiple band is only an
  anchor if the earnings base underneath it is the same kind of earnings.
- *Discriminator* (`historical_distribution`) — the band conditioned on comparable revenue
  mix, not the raw ten-year average.

## Forensic screens (sector-specific)
- **Growth reported only in INR** in a depreciating-rupee year, or the cc series quietly dropped.
- The "digital" or "next-gen" revenue definition changed, or restated, to show a higher share.
- Utilisation redefined (trainees in or out) between periods; utilisation above the ceiling
  presented as a lever with room left.
- **TCV without duration**, or the TCV definition changed; renewals counted as new wins; the same
  deal announced twice (signing and ramp).
- Sub-contractor cost rising while headcount growth is presented as capacity — margin is being
  rented and it reverses.
- Revenue per employee flat or falling while an AI or automation story is being told.
- Unbilled revenue and DSO rising faster than revenue — the classic services-sector working-capital
  tell, and often the first sign of a fixed-price project in trouble.
- Fixed-price project cost overruns recognised late; onerous-contract provisions absent from a
  book with a rising fixed-price share.
- Acquisitions used to mask organic deceleration — always ask for the organic cc series ex-M&A;
  earn-out liabilities and the acquired entities' margins undisclosed.
- Goodwill and intangibles from past deals never tested down despite a lost client.
- Capitalisation of internal software or platform-development cost.
- ESOP cost excluded from "adjusted" EBITDA in a business whose main input is people.
- Hedging gains presented inside operating margin; the hedge book's realised rate not disclosed.
- Client concentration falling only because a large account shrank.
- Attrition quoted quarterly-annualised in a good quarter and LTM in a bad one.

## Dependencies to map
Client-side technology-spend cycles by vertical (BFSI capex, US healthcare payer/provider spend,
retail discretionary IT, auto ER&D programmes for KPIT-type names) · US and EU macro, since
discretionary project spend is the first thing cut · **AI/GenAI pricing behaviour in the client base
— the sector's single largest structural uncertainty**, evidenced through deal terms rather than
vendor claims · hyperscaler and platform-vendor partnership economics (AWS/Azure/GCP, Salesforce,
SAP, ServiceNow) · USD-INR, EUR-INR, GBP-INR and the hedging policy · US visa policy (H-1B fees and
adjudication) and onshore-localisation cost · India's SEZ/STPI tax regime and the effective tax rate ·
NASSCOM industry data and Gartner/IDC spend forecasts for the addressable market ·
wage inflation and campus-hiring supply · GCC (global capability centre) expansion by clients, which
is simultaneously a competitor for talent and a source of managed-services demand · data-protection
and data-localisation regulation (DPDP, GDPR) affecting delivery models.

## Common archetypes here
`garp` (the sector's most common honest framing), `margin-expansion` (defensible when the lever
bridge is quantified — this is one sector where the mechanism is genuinely knowable),
`market-share-gainer` (tier-2 taking share, evidenced by client-bucket migration and large-deal
wins), `quality-compounder` for the genuine tier-1 franchises, `turnaround` for names repairing
growth or margin, and `re-rating`, which in IT services is usually an argument that a tier-2 deserves
tier-1 treatment — apply the 40% rule and demand the client-mining evidence. Be alert to
`cyclical-peak` dressed as structural digital demand, and to `regulatory-tailwind` framing around
PLI or GCC trends that does not reach the P&L.
