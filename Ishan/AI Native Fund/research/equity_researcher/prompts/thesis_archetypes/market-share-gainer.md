# Archetype — Market-share gainer

**Skepticism weight: 3 (medium).**

## Definition
The company grows faster than its end market by taking share, so growth does not depend
on the market's growth rate.

## Return source
Volume growth above industry, compounding into operating leverage.

## `must_be_true`
1. **Share is measured, in a stated denominator.** By volume or value, in which
   geography, against which set. An unmeasured share claim is an adjective.
2. **Share gain is already visible in the data**, over at least 2–3 years, not projected
   from a new capacity or a new product.
3. **The reason for the gain is named and durable** — distribution reach, capacity when
   rivals had none, a cost advantage that funds price, a regulatory approval others lack,
   a customer win with a stated ramp.
4. **The loser is identified.** Share is zero-sum. If nobody is losing, the "gain" is
   probably market growth or a redefinition of the denominator.
5. **The gain is not bought at a return-destroying price** — check realisation and margin
   while share rises. Share bought with discounts is a cost, not a moat.

## Standard evidence pattern
Industry volume series → company volume series → share series → the named mechanism →
competitor commentary confirming the loss → margin held or improving through the gain.

## Standard failure mode
**Denominator drift** — the market is redefined (a narrower segment, a new geography) so
share rises without anything changing. Second: **extrapolating a capacity-driven gain**
where the company simply had capacity in a tight market; when rivals add capacity, the
share reverses.

## Falsifiers
- Share flat or down for two periods while the narrative continues.
- Realisation falling faster than peers (share bought, not won).
- The named loser adds capacity or responds on price.
- Growth concentrated in one customer, making "share" a contract.

## Sectors where it recurs
Building materials, cables and wires, EMS, auto ancillaries, FMCG categories under
premiumisation, retail, diagnostics, private banks and NBFCs versus PSU incumbents.

## Corpus note
**HDB Financial Services** (ICICI Securities, Mar-2026) is instructive for the opposite
reason: it publishes an exhibit showing the company grew *slower* than peers over three
years (`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §7.3), i.e. it explicitly declines to claim this
archetype while still recommending BUY. That is condition 2 being respected rather than
fudged.
