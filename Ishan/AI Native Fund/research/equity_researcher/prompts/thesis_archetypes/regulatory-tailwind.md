# Archetype — Regulatory tailwind / PLI / import substitution

**Skepticism weight: 4 (medium-high).** Policy creates the economics, and policy is not
under the company's control.

## Definition
A government action — PLI scheme, tariff or anti-dumping duty, a capex programme, a
licensing regime, China+1 sourcing shifts, a blending mandate — changes the industry's
economics in the company's favour.

## Return source
Volume and/or margin created by policy, plus the multiple the market awards a
policy-favoured name. Frequently accompanied by a re-rating leg; decompose.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The policy is notified, not announced.** A budget speech, a draft scheme and a
   gazetted notification are three different things. Cite the instrument.
2. **The company's eligibility is confirmed and quantified** — approved under the scheme,
   for what capacity, with what incentive, over what period, subject to what investment
   and output thresholds.
3. **The benefit is translated into rupees per unit or per year**, then compared against
   the earnings base so the reader can see how much of the thesis it is.
4. **The expiry, renewal or step-down is stated.** Anti-dumping duties lapse. PLI has a
   tenor. A tailwind with an end date is an annuity, not a moat, and should not earn a
   compounder multiple.
5. **Competitor access is assessed.** If every peer gets the same benefit, it accrues to
   the *customer* through price, not to the company through margin. This is the condition
   most often skipped.

## Standard evidence pattern
The notification with date → eligibility approval → incentive quantified per unit and per
year → capex/output obligations → the P&L line it lands in → tenor and step-down schedule
→ list of peers with the same benefit.

## Standard failure mode
**Treating an industry-wide subsidy as a company-specific advantage.** Second:
**capitalising a temporary incentive into a permanent multiple** — awarding a compounder
rating to earnings that expire with the scheme.

## Falsifiers
- The scheme's disbursement slips or is renegotiated (common).
- Duties lapse without renewal.
- Peers commission the same capacity, competing the benefit away in price.
- Output or investment thresholds are missed, forfeiting eligibility.

## Sectors where it recurs
Solar and renewables, EMS, specialty chemicals and API (China+1), defence
(indigenisation lists), sugar (ethanol blending), textiles (RoSCTL), steel and aluminium
(anti-dumping), power transmission (national capex programmes).

## Corpus examples
- **APAR Industries** (Nuvama, Jan-2025) — "ample re-rating potential for the company
  given GoI's singular focus on T&D". A genuine tailwind, but condition 5 is unaddressed:
  every conductor and cable maker benefits from the same T&D capex, so the note needed to
  show why APAR captures disproportionate value. Its segment-anchored SOTP is otherwise
  the strongest valuation work in the corpus.
- **Waaree Energies** (Yes Securities, Sep-2025) — domestic content rules and module
  demand; condition 4 (tenor) and condition 5 (peer access, in a rapidly expanding
  domestic module industry) are the pressure points.
