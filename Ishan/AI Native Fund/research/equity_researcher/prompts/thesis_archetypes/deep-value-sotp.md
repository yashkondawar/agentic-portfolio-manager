# Archetype — Deep value / SOTP

**Skepticism weight: 4 (high).** Cheapness is not a catalyst.

## Definition
The market price sits below the value of the parts, below replacement cost, or below a
conservative liquidation/asset value. The claim is that the gap is unwarranted.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The value is computed on a basis a buyer would actually pay** — realised transaction
   comparables, replacement cost with a credible per-unit build cost, or peer multiples on
   the parts. Book value is an accounting artefact, not a valuation.
2. **The cause of the discount is named** — the same condition as `re-rating.md` #2, and
   for the same reason.
3. **A mechanism converts value into shareholder return.** Deep value without a catalyst
   is a permanent condition. Acceptable mechanisms: a sale, a demerger, a buyback, a
   dividend policy change, a control change, an activist, or the asset simply starting to
   earn.
4. **The assets earn, or can be made to earn, their cost of capital.** Assets that
   destroy value are correctly discounted; buying them cheaply just buys the destruction
   more slowly.
5. **Cash burn is bounded.** Time is the enemy of a value thesis; if the company consumes
   cash while waiting, the discount widens with every quarter.

## Standard evidence pattern
Asset-by-asset valuation on transaction comps → replacement cost per unit with the source
→ the discount quantified → its named cause → the catalyst → returns on the assets →
the cash burn/generation profile while waiting.

## Standard failure mode
**The value trap** — a persistently cheap company whose discount reflects a real,
permanent flaw (governance, control, terminal decline). Second: **replacement cost
without demand** — replacement cost is a ceiling only if someone would build the asset
today; in an oversupplied industry, nobody would.

## Falsifiers
- No catalyst progress after 12 months and the discount unchanged.
- Assets sold below the assumed value.
- Cash burn erodes more value than the discount offers.
- Promoter or control behaviour reconfirms the discount's cause.

## Sectors where it recurs
Holdcos, real estate land banks, hotels (replacement cost), sugar and paper (asset-heavy
commodity), PSUs, cash-rich small caps with entrenched promoters.

## Corpus example
**SAMHI Hotels** (Yes Securities, Sep-2025) uses replacement cost the *right* way — not as
a passive "it's cheap" claim at the valuation step, but as an **acquisition discipline**
applied at the buying step, which is what makes the resulting ROCE repeatable
(`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §7.9). That converts condition 1 from a valuation assertion
into an operating rule, and condition 4 is answered by the ROCE that the entry discount
creates.
