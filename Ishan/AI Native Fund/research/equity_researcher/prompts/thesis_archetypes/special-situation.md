# Archetype — Special situation (demerger, holdco, asset monetisation)

**Skepticism weight: 4 (high).** The value is real; the *timing* almost never is.

## Definition
A corporate action — demerger, spin-off, listing of a subsidiary, holdco discount
collapse, InvIT/REIT monetisation, buyback, open offer — releases value the current
structure obscures.

## `must_be_true`
1. **The event is announced, with a scheme and a regulatory path.** Board approval, NCLT
   filing, SEBI/exchange approvals, record date. An event that is merely "expected" is not
   a thesis; it is a hope with a valuation attached.
2. **The value released is computed both ways** — SOTP before and after, with the discount
   explicitly stated. Show what the parts are worth on peer multiples and what the market
   currently pays for the whole.
3. **The discount's cause is named** (see `re-rating.md` condition 2): tax leakage,
   promoter control, cross-holdings, minority-unfriendly history, illiquidity. Structural
   causes survive the event.
4. **The timeline is dated with the realistic Indian regulatory clock**, not the
   management-guided one. NCLT schemes routinely take 12–24 months.
5. **Tax and leakage are modelled.** Value released ≠ value received.

## Standard evidence pattern
Current structure diagram → SOTP by entity with peer-anchored multiples → holdco discount
history for the same group and comparable Indian holdcos → the scheme's regulatory
milestones with dates → tax treatment → post-event ownership and float.

## Standard failure mode
**Assuming the discount fully collapses on the event.** Indian holdco discounts are
persistent and often re-form in the successor entity. Second: **the perpetual
demerger** — announced, then delayed for years, with the thesis rolled forward each time.

## Falsifiers
- A regulatory milestone slips by more than two quarters.
- The scheme is restructured in a way that changes the entity split.
- A comparable Indian demerger completes and the discount does *not* close.
- Promoter behaviour after the event repeats the pattern that caused the discount.

## Sectors where it recurs
Conglomerates and holdcos, real estate (InvIT/REIT), infrastructure developers (asset
recycling), PSUs (disinvestment), pharma (API/formulation splits), hotels (ITC Hotels-style
demergers).

## Corpus example
**Tata Chemicals** (JM Financial, Oct-2025) applies the discipline properly on the
valuation side: a per-leg SOTP with an explicit **holding-company discount** on the listed
Rallis stake — the discount is modelled rather than assumed away — and the implied blended
multiples published as a sanity check on the parts. Full figures at
`docs/ER_CORPUS_FINDINGS.md` §7.7; the sector treatment is in
`prompts/sector_playbooks/specialty_chemicals.md`.
